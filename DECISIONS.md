# Decisions

Choices made during unattended runs, with the reason. Newest at the bottom.

- **D1 (2026-11-10). Python 3.12 through uv.** The system Python is 3.9, too old for current geospatial packages. uv installed its own CPython 3.12.14 into `.venv`.
- **D2 (2026-11-10). Analysis grid on the native Landsat lattice.** All Nairobi Landsat scenes are WRS-2 path 168 row 061, delivered in UTM 37N (EPSG:32637). UTM 37S (EPSG:32737) is the same projection with a false northing 10,000,000 m larger, so pixels are read in their native lattice and relabelled in EPSG:32737 by adding 10,000,000 m to y. No resampling of surface temperature happens. Grid: 1,784 × 1,180 cells of 30 m, county plus 2 km, `data/interim/grid_30m.tif`.
- **D3 (2026-11-10). County boundary licence.** geoBoundaries lists KEN ADM1 as Public Domain (source RCMRD), not CC BY 4.0 as prompt 01 expected. I followed the provider's metadata.
- **D4 (2026-11-10). Wards from geoBoundaries ADM3.** This layer (Omare and Omare, CC BY 4.0) gives exactly 85 wards inside Nairobi when wards are selected by representative point. I did not try HDX first, because geoBoundaries returned the right count and an open licence on the first attempt. Wards are clipped to the county outline; they cover 674.6 of 705.3 km² because the two sources' edges differ slightly. Ward names were title-cased with roman numerals restored (for example "Dandora Area II"); "Sarangombe" is the source spelling of Sarang'ombe.
- **D5 (2026-11-10). Cool-season composite reaches back to 2018.** Only 2 usable June to August scenes exist from 2023 on (the long rains and cool season are cloudy), so the "extend back until 4 scenes" rule took it to 2018: 4 scenes from 2018, 2020, 2024 and 2026. The hot-season composite has 12 scenes, all from 2023 to 2026.
- **D6 (2026-11-10). WorldPop 2025 constrained (R2025A v1).** The newest Kenya release covers 2015 to 2030. I chose 2025 because it is closest to the 2023 to 2026 surface temperature composites. It is a modelled estimate. Population is moved to the 30 m grid as density (people per m²) and back to counts, so totals are kept (county 5,653,421 native, 5,657,304 on the grid).
- **D7 (2026-11-10). GHSL on a 90 m grid.** GHS-BUILT-S 100 m (Mollweide) was regridded bilinearly to a 90 m grid nested in the 30 m grid. Kept epochs 1975, 1990, 2000, 2010, 2020 and 2025. 2025 is a JRC projection, so the story uses 1990 vs 2020 as observed epochs.
- **D8 (2026-11-10). Buildings file not committed.** `nairobi_buildings.parquet` is 92 MB (746,499 footprints), over the 50 MB limit in CLAUDE.md, so it is git-ignored and rebuilt by `src/pipeline/s3_context.py`.

- **D12 (2026-11-10). Comparison areas for the CBD and Industrial Area.** OpenStreetMap has no single polygon for either. CBD = 750 m circle around -1.2840, 36.8240 (between Moi Avenue and Tom Mboya Street, chosen to keep Uhuru and Central Parks out). Industrial Area = 1 km circle around the OSM `place=suburb` point "Industrial Area". Karura Forest and Nairobi Arboretum use their OSM `landuse=forest` polygons. - **D14 (2026-11-10). Park rings use built-up cells only** (WorldCover built-up share at least 50 %, tree share under 30 %), outside every OSM park, forest or reserve, compared with the 700 to 1,000 m band. "Reach" = first ring within 0.5 °C of that band.

- **D34 (2026-11-10, v2). Tiles.** WebP XYZ tiles made in Python (`src/web/tiles.py`): values are resampled into each Web Mercator tile (area average when zoomed out, bilinear when zoomed in) and then classed, so class edges are smooth curves instead of 30 m or 100 m squares; land cover uses mode and nearest neighbour. County layers z9 to 15, GHSL z9 to 14, Sentinel-2 z9 to 16; the map overzooms with linear resampling. Cells with no clear reading in any scene stay transparent (0.4 % of the county); they are not filled. Absolute surface temperature uses breaks at the county's 10th, 20th, 40th, 60th, 80th and 90th percentiles (seven classes need six breaks). Population uses quintile breaks of populated 100 m cells, with under 100 people per km² left transparent. Growth counts a cell as built-up from 25 % built surface. Vector tiles: tippecanoe directories, uncompressed; tippecanoe skips empty tiles, so empty positions inside the county bounds get zero-byte `.pbf` files (a valid empty tile) to avoid 404s. Vite runs in `appType: 'mpa'` so a missing file returns 404 instead of `index.html`.
- **D35 (2026-11-10, v2). Diverging ramp check.** Checked with `outputs/qa/check_diverging_ramp.py` (CIELAB, and colour-blindness simulation after Machado et al. 2009 at full severity). Lightness L* rises from each end to the neutral midpoint (31, 50, 73, 87, **95**, 85, 74, 53, 35), so the middle class reads as "about the same". Smallest colour difference between neighbouring classes: ΔE 14.3 (normal vision), 12.3 (protanopia), 14.7 (deuteranopia), 14.9 (tritanopia). The −4 to −6 class and the +4 to +6 class stay far apart: ΔE 96.9 normal, 66.9 protanopia, 84.5 deuteranopia. All checks pass, so no steps changed. Class shares over the county: 45.0 % blue, 10.8 % neutral, 44.2 % orange or red, which passes legibility rule 2.

- **D37 (2026-11-10, v2). Animations play by themselves (exception to "no autoplay").** Stephane asked for moving maps like NASA's wind animation (design brief section 5). - **D38 (2026-11-10, v2). "A day of heat" uses CLMS hourly LST, sharpened with Landsat.** `CDSE_CLIENT_ID` and `CDSE_CLIENT_SECRET` are set in `.env` (checked by a script printing only "set" or "missing"), so I took the CLMS path: Global Land Surface Temperature hourly v3 (~3 km), Sentinel Hub collection `byoc-12225aec-26dd-4e2c-bbfd-994c253c1ba8`, for 27 January 2026, the hottest clear Landsat morning of the 2026 hot season. Sharpening is my own implementation: hourly CLMS interpolated to 30 m, plus the same morning's Landsat residual (Landsat minus its average over each CLMS cell) scaled by the day's heating (0 at dawn, 1 at the overpass hour). The Zenodo record for Zlinszky (2026) could not be retrieved through search, so I followed only the general idea named in the prompt and cite it as given. Check at the overpass hour: CLMS minus Landsat averaged to the CLMS cells, bias −1.75 °C, RMSE 2.69 °C, r 0.88 (190 cells). One hour (15:00 Nairobi time) is mostly cloud. Where that morning's Landsat scene was cloudy, no fine detail is added; where CLMS is cloudy, the map shows no colour. ERA5-Land was not needed.
- **D39 (2026-11-10, v2). Calendar heatmap without numbers in its cells.** The brief asks for numbers printed in each heatmap cell. That works for the two tables (17 × 6 and 20 × 5 cells), but the calendar has about 1,400 days in cells of 8 to 20 px, where numbers cannot be read. Its values are given by tap, click or the arrow keys in a line under the calendar, and the data is downloadable as CSV. The calendar's diverging steps are narrower (±0.25, 1, 2, 3 °C) than the map's, because daily air temperature varies less than surface temperature.
- **D40 (2026-11-10, v2). Sentinel-2 composite.** Sentinel-2 L2A from Planetary Computer, January to March 2026, 11 days with under 40 % scene cloud (23 tiles), median of pixels the scene classification marks as vegetation, bare soil or water, offset of 1,000 removed (processing baseline 04.00 and later). Stretch: 1st to 99.5th percentile per band, gamma 1.45 (true colour) and 1.1 (false colour, bands 12, 8A, 4). The first, dask-based version stalled on network reads; the second reads one day at a time. The 10 m GeoTIFFs (45 to 141 MB) are git-ignored and rebuilt by `src/pipeline/s8_sentinel2.py`; the web tiles are committed.
- **D41 (2026-11-10, v2). What changed against v1's look, after comparing with `nasa/08` and `ours_v1/02`.** (1) Heat shown as a difference from the city median on the diverging ramp (D35), so about 45 % of the county is blue and 44 % orange or red, instead of one red ramp. (4) Opacity 0.9 over Light and 0.75 over imagery: at 0.75 the street pattern shows through without washing out the ramp. (5) Population moved from grey blocks to a smooth purple quintile ramp; growth shows only new built-up land in red over the 1990 city in grey. (6) Legends are vertical lists or a diverging strip with five ticks, so labels cannot collide. The `nasa/08` look (crisp categorical colours, readable place names over data) is matched on land cover and heat. ## D53 (2026-11-14). Nairobi school locations and heat at schools

- **Sources.** The Ministry of Education school list as published by the World Bank on energydata.info (CC BY 4.0; records marked "Ministry of Education, 2016" and "Open Data Kenya, School 2007"), read from the dataset's public vector tiles at zoom 10 because the file downloads sit behind a bot check (two attempts). Tiles carry every attribute, including exact coordinates; no cluster attributes appear and the largest tile is under tippecanoe's 500 kB limit, so clustering most likely dropped no points. OpenStreetMap through Overpass (amenity=school and amenity=kindergarten; nodes, ways and relations as centres; 14 November 2026). The HOT education export on HDX and Open Schools Kenya are OpenStreetMap extracts and were not merged. opendata.go.ke 2007 was a dead link. UNICEF Giga needs an API key (HTTP 401 twice).
- **Old datum.** Part of both official lists is in the Arc 1960 datum: of the 2007 records matched to OpenStreetMap, 109 line up only after the Arc 1960 to WGS84 shift (+93 m east, −286 m north at Nairobi), against 110 that line up as given (2016 list: 58 and 157). Official points are matched at both positions, and the 1,480 schools found only in an official list are flagged "may be up to about 300 m off".
- **Merge rules.** Within 50 m with name similarity at least 0.75 (difflib, generic words removed), or from different sources with similarity at least 0.9 within 500 m; names must share a distinctive word. Groups are never joined when any pair conflicts: levels both known and different, Old against New, Girls against Boys, Academy against Primary/Secondary/High, ECD or nursery against Primary/Secondary/High, or the same source more than 150 m apart. Result: 3,797 places, 3,714 schools for children; at most about 142 duplicates remain.
- **Completeness.** Above the registered counts reported by The Eastleigh Voice (28 March 2024) for primary (2,058 against 991) and secondary (536 against 314), because informal and low-cost schools mapped in OpenStreetMap, closed 2007-list schools and some duplicates are included; below for pre-primary (769 against 1,168 ECDE centres), because ECDE units inside primary compounds are rarely mapped separately. Described as a broad map of school sites, not a register.
- **Heat.** Median of the 30 m hot-season cells within 100 m of each school; "on the hottest fifth" means at or above the county 80th percentile (41.65 °C, the q4 threshold). Headline counts are schools for children; 83 adult, vocational and tertiary places are excluded. A random 300 schools were recomputed with a true 100 m circle and rasterio mask; all agree within 0.03 °C. Code: `src/pipeline/s10_schools.py`, `src/analysis/schools.py` (now also merges its `schools` key into `analysis/results_extra.json`). Landsat passes at about 10:43, during the school day; values are ground, roof and road temperature, not classroom air temperature.

## D55 (2026-11-14). WorldPop scaled to census sub-counties (task 5, not used on the site)

- Each of the 85 wards is assigned to a census sub-county by name, with a confidence level (73 high: the ward is, or is inside, a census location or sub-location; 12 medium: the ward lies between named units), in `src/analysis/census_scaling.py`. Where both exist, this agrees with the stricter D54 link for all 63 wards it covers (Mowlem was first assigned to Embakasi and corrected to Njiru, where the census lists sub-location Mowlem).
- Census sub-county totals are grown from census night 2019 to mid-2025 at Nairobi's intercensal rate, 3.43 % a year (KNBS counts 3,138,369 in 2009 and 4,397,073 in 2019), factor 1.2181, giving 5,356,025 for the county against WorldPop's ward sum of 5,588,514. Ward WorldPop totals are multiplied by each census sub-county's factor (Njiru 0.588 to Kamukunji 1.369).
- Result: this does not fix Woodley/Kenyatta Golf Course. It rises from 133,937 to 154,593, because Kibra as a whole is scaled up. The census itself counted 36,115 people in Woodley division, whose 9.6 km² almost equals the ward's 9.5 km². The problem is how WorldPop spreads people inside Kibra (Laini Saba: census 29,605 against WorldPop 6,032; Lindi 35,618 against 11,920; Sarang'ombe 55,303 against 25,109). Census locations and sub-locations line up with wards closely in Kibra and Kangemi, so scaling at location level is the recommended next step. One growth rate for the whole county also understates growth on the fringe (Njiru) and overstates it in the centre.

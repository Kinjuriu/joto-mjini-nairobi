# Decisions

Choices made during unattended runs, with the reason. Newest at the bottom.

- **D1 (2026-11-10). Python 3.12 through uv.** The system Python is 3.9, too old for current geospatial packages. uv installed its own CPython 3.12.14 into `.venv`.
- **D2 (2026-11-10). Analysis grid on the native Landsat lattice.** All Nairobi Landsat scenes are WRS-2 path 168 row 061, delivered in UTM 37N (EPSG:32637). UTM 37S (EPSG:32737) is the same projection with a false northing 10,000,000 m larger, so pixels are read in their native lattice and relabelled in EPSG:32737 by adding 10,000,000 m to y. No resampling of surface temperature happens. Grid: 1,784 × 1,180 cells of 30 m, county plus 2 km, `data/interim/grid_30m.tif`.
- **D3 (2026-11-10). County boundary licence.** geoBoundaries lists KEN ADM1 as Public Domain (source RCMRD), not CC BY 4.0 as prompt 01 expected. I followed the provider's metadata.
- **D4 (2026-11-10). Wards from geoBoundaries ADM3.** This layer (Omare and Omare, CC BY 4.0) gives exactly 85 wards inside Nairobi when wards are selected by representative point. I did not try HDX first, because geoBoundaries returned the right count and an open licence on the first attempt. Wards are clipped to the county outline; they cover 674.6 of 705.3 km² because the two sources' edges differ slightly. Ward names were title-cased with roman numerals restored (for example "Dandora Area II"); "Sarangombe" is the source spelling of Sarang'ombe.
- **D5 (2026-11-10). Cool-season composite reaches back to 2018.** Only 2 usable June to August scenes exist from 2023 on (the long rains and cool season are cloudy), so the "extend back until 4 scenes" rule took it to 2018: 4 scenes from 2018, 2020, 2024 and 2026. The hot-season composite has 12 scenes, all from 2023 to 2026.
- **D6 (2026-11-10). WorldPop 2025 constrained (R2025A v1).** The newest Kenya release covers 2015 to 2030. I chose 2025 because it is closest to the 2023 to 2026 surface temperature composites. It is a modelled estimate. Population is moved to the 30 m grid as density (people per m²) and back to counts, so totals are kept (county 5,653,421 native, 5,657,304 on the grid).
- **D7 (2026-11-10). GHSL on a 90 m grid.** GHS-BUILT-S 100 m (Mollweide) was regridded bilinearly to a 90 m grid nested in the 30 m grid. Kept epochs 1975, 1990, 2000, 2010, 2020 and 2025. 2025 is a JRC projection, so the story uses 1990 vs 2020 as observed epochs.
- **D8 (2026-11-10). Buildings file not committed.** `nairobi_buildings.parquet` is 92 MB (746,499 footprints), over the 50 MB limit in CLAUDE.md, so it is git-ignored and rebuilt by `src/pipeline/s3_context.py`.
- **D9 (2026-11-10). No informal-settlement layer tonight.** WRI's GIULU irregular settlements layer is only on Google Earth Engine (needs an account) and Cool Cities Lab offers no downloads. Question 7's settlement comparison is skipped; see QUESTIONS.md.
- **D10 (2026-11-10). Raster tiles through MBTiles.** Homebrew GDAL 3.13.3 has a PMTiles driver for vector data only, so raster layers will be made with GDAL's MBTiles driver and converted with `pmtiles convert`.
- **D11 (2026-11-10). Kisumu and CLMS sharpening skipped.** Both are optional. CLMS needs Copernicus credentials, and there is no `.env`.
- **D12 (2026-11-10). Comparison areas for the CBD and Industrial Area.** OpenStreetMap has no single polygon for either. CBD = 750 m circle around -1.2840, 36.8240 (between Moi Avenue and Tom Mboya Street, chosen to keep Uhuru and Central Parks out). Industrial Area = 1 km circle around the OSM `place=suburb` point "Industrial Area". Karura Forest and Nairobi Arboretum use their OSM `landuse=forest` polygons. The shapes are saved in `data/processed/comparison_areas.geojson`. Stephane may prefer other shapes (QUESTIONS 3).
- **D13 (2026-11-10). "Mostly built-up" and "mostly tree-covered" mean at least 75 % of the 30 m cell** in WorldCover 2021. Tree bands are 10 % steps.
- **D14 (2026-11-10). Park rings use built-up cells only** (WorldCover built-up share at least 50 %, tree share under 30 %), outside every OSM park, forest or reserve, compared with the 700 to 1,000 m band. "Reach" = first ring within 0.5 °C of that band.
- **D15 (2026-11-10). Inequality groups are fifths of wards (17 each)** ranked by population-weighted surface temperature. Temperatures are population-weighted; tree and built-up shares are area-weighted.
- **D16 (2026-11-10). El Niño period starts 1 May 2026,** the centre month of the first RONI season at or above +0.5 °C (AMJ 2026).
- **D17 (2026-11-10). Raster tiles stop at zoom 13** (about 19 m pixels, already finer than the 30 m grid); MapLibre overzooms beyond that with nearest-neighbour resampling. Colours are baked in with fixed breaks: surface temperature 24, 28, 32, 35, 38, 41, 44, 48 °C (values beyond either end take the end colour); difference from the median −12, −4, −1.5, 1.5, 4, 12 °C; tree cover and GHSL 10, 25, 50, 75, 100 % (under 10 % transparent); population 1,000, 5,000, 15,000, 30,000 people per km² (under 1,000 transparent).
- **D18 (2026-11-10). Building tiles.** tippecanoe 2.79 exited with status 101 on FlatGeobuf input and with `--extend-zooms-if-still-dropping`/`--coalesce-densest-as-needed`; it works on GeoJSONSeq with `--drop-smallest-as-needed`. Zooms 12 to 16, 43.5 MB. At zooms 12 and 13 only the larger footprints are kept; every footprint is present from zoom 14. Each building carries the surface temperature of the 30 m cell at its representative point (which itself reflects about 100 m around it).
- **D19 (2026-11-10). Repaired Node.** `brew install gdal` upgraded Homebrew's `simdjson`, which broke the installed Node 26.5.0 (missing `libsimdjson.33.dylib`). I ran `brew upgrade node` (now 26.13.0, npm 11.20.0). This changes your global Node version.
- **D20 (2026-11-10). Corrected a chart title.** The land-cover chart first said built-up and bare ground were hottest; the data shows dry grassland, farmland and bare ground were as hot as or hotter than built-up land in January to March. The title now says trees and water stayed coolest (F5).
- **D21 (2026-11-10). Web layers stop at the county edge.** The 2 km buffer is kept for analysis only; web rasters are masked to the county so ward lines, legends and colours describe the same area. Earlier tile builds were moved to `data/interim/_old_*.pmtiles` (git-ignored), not deleted.
- **D22 (2026-11-10). Built-up (GHSL) maps use three classes** (5 to 25 %, 25 to 50 %, over 50 % of the cell built) and the three darker steps of the fixed neutral ramp. With four classes and the lightest step, growth was invisible on the grey basemap. This replaces the GHSL part of D17.
- **D23 (2026-11-10). Numbers enter the site only through `analysis/results.json`.** A Vite plugin fills `{{path|format}}` placeholders at build time and fails on an unknown path. This guarantees the copy matches FINDINGS.md.
- **D24 (2026-11-10). Narrow-screen scrollytelling uses one sticky map** at the top 46 % of the screen with cards scrolling beneath, instead of a map above every card (see SITE_REPORT.md, "Things I am unsure about").
- **D25 (2026-11-10). Basemap is Protomaps "grayscale" with a paper background,** so colour belongs to the data. Glyphs and sprites are self-hosted for offline use at the workshop.
- **D26 (2026-11-10). Scroll fallback for chapters.** Scrollama missed steps 2 and 7 when the page jumped past them; a small scroll handler applies the step under the trigger line too.
- **D27 (2026-11-10). Building layer id is `bldg-lst`.** The Protomaps basemap already has a layer called `buildings`.
- **D28 (2026-11-10). Optional Kisumu run uses the same code with `JOTO_CITY=kisumu`.** Outputs go to `data/*/kisumu/`; Nairobi paths are unchanged. Kisumu is in UTM 36S (EPSG:32736). The geoBoundaries county polygon includes part of Lake Victoria (2,675 km²), so the Kisumu study area is the union of its 35 wards (2,060 km², land only); the original outline is kept as `kisumu_boundary_with_lake.geojson`. Kisumu needs three Landsat path/rows (169/060, 169/061, 170/060), so a scene is usable if it covers at least 30 % of the study area and at least 80 % of the covered part is clear (for Nairobi this rule gives the same result as before, because every scene covers the whole county). WorldCover is now mosaicked from all tiles that intersect the study area.
- **D29 (2026-11-10, v2). Wards now come from the IEBC-based HDX layer.** "Administrative Wards in Kenya 1450" (American Red Cross, HDX, CC BY) has exactly 85 Nairobi wards in 17 sub-counties whose names match the IEBC constituencies; every ward name matches the IEBC 2013 election layer (Maron) except Ngei, which that layer lacks. The Maron layer itself sits about 1 km south-west of the population and buildings, so it was used for names only. The ward union covers 695.1 of 705.3 km² (symmetric difference 4.6 % of the county). **The Woodley problem remains:** under these lines Woodley/Kenyatta Golf Course still holds 133,937 WorldPop people (68 % of Kibra sub-county), while the layer's own 2009 census count is 35,355. Building footprints show that part of Kibera's dense fabric does fall inside Woodley under these lines (4,310 buildings under 60 m², more than any other Kibra ward), so the lines are not obviously wrong; most of the gap looks like WorldPop's allocation. Woodley stays in the coolest fifth of wards (F8). Listed in QUESTIONS.md.
- **D30 (2026-11-10, v2). Comparison areas replaced (F3).** City centre = the Nairobi Central ward polygon (3.15 km²; Uhuru Park, Central Park and City Park all lie outside it). Industrial Area = 10 touching OSM `landuse=industrial` polygons merged (4.3 km², ids in `analysis/tables/industrial_area_osm_ids.csv`). Karura and the Arboretum unchanged. **F3 old → new:** city centre 38.8 → 39.1 °C; Industrial Area 41.3 → 41.5 °C; both together 40.5 → 40.7 °C; Karura and Arboretum 27.6 °C (unchanged); gap 12.8 → 13.1 °C. The v1 circles are in `data/processed/_old/comparison_areas_v1.geojson`.
- **D31 (2026-11-10, v2). Roof check result: caveat applies (F16).** The Landsat emissivity used in the five dense, almost treeless wards (median 0.966) is the same as the county median (0.965) and higher than Nairobi Central (0.960). The product therefore does not account for metal roofs there, and those wards may be hotter than shown. Each 0.01 of emissivity overestimated lowers the reading by about 0.7 °C. The web ward layer carries `roof_caveat = true` for wards with at least 75 % built-up and under 5 % tree cover (32 wards), and the ward cards and explorer show the caveat for them.

# Decisions

Choices made during unattended runs, with the reason. Newest at the bottom.

- **D1 (2026-11-10). Python 3.12 through uv.** The system Python is 3.9, too old for current geospatial packages. uv installed its own CPython 3.12.14 into `.venv`.
- **D2 (2026-11-10). Analysis grid on the native Landsat lattice.** All Nairobi Landsat scenes are WRS-2 path 168 row 061, delivered in UTM 37N (EPSG:32637). UTM 37S (EPSG:32737) is the same projection with a false northing 10,000,000 m larger, so pixels are read in their native lattice and relabelled in EPSG:32737 by adding 10,000,000 m to y. No resampling of surface temperature happens. Grid: 1,784 × 1,180 cells of 30 m, county plus 2 km, `data/interim/grid_30m.tif`.
- **D3 (2026-11-10). County boundary licence.** geoBoundaries lists KEN ADM1 as Public Domain (source RCMRD), not CC BY 4.0 as prompt 01 expected. I followed the provider's metadata.
- **D4 (2026-11-10). Wards from geoBoundaries ADM3.** This layer (Omare and Omare, CC BY 4.0) gives exactly 85 wards inside Nairobi when wards are selected by representative point. I did not try HDX first, because geoBoundaries returned the right count and an open licence on the first attempt. Wards are clipped to the county outline; they cover 674.6 of 705.3 km² because the two sources' edges differ slightly. Ward names were title-cased with roman numerals restored (for example "Dandora Area II"); "Sarangombe" is the source spelling of Sarang'ombe.
- **D5 (2026-11-10). Cool-season composite reaches back to 2018.** Only 2 usable June to August scenes exist from 2023 on (the long rains and cool season are cloudy), so the "extend back until 4 scenes" rule took it to 2018: 4 scenes from 2018, 2020, 2024 and 2026. The hot-season composite has 12 scenes, all from 2023 to 2026.
- **D6 (2026-11-10). WorldPop 2025 constrained (R2025A v1).** The newest Kenya release covers 2015 to 2030. I chose 2025 because it is closest to the 2023 to 2026 surface temperature composites. It is a modelled estimate. Population is moved to the 30 m grid as density (people per m²) and back to counts, so totals are kept (county 5,653,421 native, 5,657,304 on the grid).
- **D7 (2026-11-10). GHSL on a 90 m grid.** GHS-BUILT-S 100 m (Mollweide) was regridded bilinearly to a 90 m grid nested in the 30 m grid. Kept epochs 1975, 1990, 2000, 2010, 2020 and 2025. 2025 is a JRC projection, so the story uses 1990 vs 2020 as observed epochs.
- **D8 (2026-11-10). Buildings file not committed.** `nairobi_buildings.parquet` is 92 MB (746,499 footprints), over the 50 MB limit in CLAUDE.md, so it is git-ignored and rebuilt by `src/pipeline/s3_context.py`.
- **D9 (2026-11-10). No informal-settlement layer tonight.** WRI's GIULU irregular settlements layer is only on Google Earth Engine (needs an account) and Cool Cities Lab offers no downloads. Question 7's settlement comparison is skipped; see QUESTIONS.md.
- **D10 (2026-11-10). Raster tiles through MBTiles.** Homebrew GDAL 3.13.3 has a PMTiles driver for vector data only, so raster layers will be made with GDAL's MBTiles driver and converted with `pmtiles convert`.
- **D11 (2026-11-10). Kisumu and CLMS sharpening skipped.** Both are optional. CLMS needs Copernicus credentials, and there is no `.env`.
- **D12 (2026-11-10). Comparison areas for the CBD and Industrial Area.** OpenStreetMap has no single polygon for either. CBD = 750 m circle around -1.2840, 36.8240 (between Moi Avenue and Tom Mboya Street, chosen to keep Uhuru and Central Parks out). Industrial Area = 1 km circle around the OSM `place=suburb` point "Industrial Area". Karura Forest and Nairobi Arboretum use their OSM `landuse=forest` polygons. The shapes are saved in `data/processed/comparison_areas.geojson`. Stephane may prefer other shapes (QUESTIONS 3).
- **D13 (2026-11-10). "Mostly built-up" and "mostly tree-covered" mean at least 75 % of the 30 m cell** in WorldCover 2021. Tree bands are 10 % steps.
- **D14 (2026-11-10). Park rings use built-up cells only** (WorldCover built-up share at least 50 %, tree share under 30 %), outside every OSM park, forest or reserve, compared with the 700 to 1,000 m band. "Reach" = first ring within 0.5 °C of that band.
- **D15 (2026-11-10). Inequality groups are fifths of wards (17 each)** ranked by population-weighted surface temperature. Temperatures are population-weighted; tree and built-up shares are area-weighted.
- **D16 (2026-11-10). El Niño period starts 1 May 2026,** the centre month of the first RONI season at or above +0.5 °C (AMJ 2026).
- **D17 (2026-11-10). Raster tiles stop at zoom 13** (about 19 m pixels, already finer than the 30 m grid); MapLibre overzooms beyond that with nearest-neighbour resampling. Colours are baked in with fixed breaks: surface temperature 24, 28, 32, 35, 38, 41, 44, 48 °C (values beyond either end take the end colour); difference from the median −12, −4, −1.5, 1.5, 4, 12 °C; tree cover and GHSL 10, 25, 50, 75, 100 % (under 10 % transparent); population 1,000, 5,000, 15,000, 30,000 people per km² (under 1,000 transparent).
- **D18 (2026-11-10). Building tiles.** tippecanoe 2.79 exited with status 101 on FlatGeobuf input and with `--extend-zooms-if-still-dropping`/`--coalesce-densest-as-needed`; it works on GeoJSONSeq with `--drop-smallest-as-needed`. Zooms 12 to 16, 43.5 MB. At zooms 12 and 13 only the larger footprints are kept; every footprint is present from zoom 14. Each building carries the surface temperature of the 30 m cell at its representative point (which itself reflects about 100 m around it).
- **D19 (2026-11-10). Repaired Node.** `brew install gdal` upgraded Homebrew's `simdjson`, which broke the installed Node 26.5.0 (missing `libsimdjson.33.dylib`). I ran `brew upgrade node` (now 26.13.0, npm 11.20.0). This changes your global Node version.
- **D20 (2026-11-10). Corrected a chart title.** The land-cover chart first said built-up and bare ground were hottest; the data shows dry grassland, farmland and bare ground were as hot as or hotter than built-up land in January to March. The title now says trees and water stayed coolest (F5).
- **D21 (2026-11-10). Web layers stop at the county edge.** The 2 km buffer is kept for analysis only; web rasters are masked to the county so ward lines, legends and colours describe the same area. Earlier tile builds were moved to `data/interim/_old_*.pmtiles` (git-ignored), not deleted.
- **D22 (2026-11-10). Built-up (GHSL) maps use three classes** (5 to 25 %, 25 to 50 %, over 50 % of the cell built) and the three darker steps of the fixed neutral ramp. With four classes and the lightest step, growth was invisible on the grey basemap. This replaces the GHSL part of D17.
- **D23 (2026-11-10). Numbers enter the site only through `analysis/results.json`.** A Vite plugin fills `{{path|format}}` placeholders at build time and fails on an unknown path. This guarantees the copy matches FINDINGS.md.
- **D24 (2026-11-10). Narrow-screen scrollytelling uses one sticky map** at the top 46 % of the screen with cards scrolling beneath, instead of a map above every card (see SITE_REPORT.md, "Things I am unsure about").
- **D25 (2026-11-10). Basemap is Protomaps "grayscale" with a paper background,** so colour belongs to the data. Glyphs and sprites are self-hosted for offline use at the workshop.
- **D26 (2026-11-10). Scroll fallback for chapters.** Scrollama missed steps 2 and 7 when the page jumped past them; a small scroll handler applies the step under the trigger line too.
- **D27 (2026-11-10). Building layer id is `bldg-lst`.** The Protomaps basemap already has a layer called `buildings`.
- **D28 (2026-11-10). Optional Kisumu run uses the same code with `JOTO_CITY=kisumu`.** Outputs go to `data/*/kisumu/`; Nairobi paths are unchanged. Kisumu is in UTM 36S (EPSG:32736). The geoBoundaries county polygon includes part of Lake Victoria (2,675 km²), so the Kisumu study area is the union of its 35 wards (2,060 km², land only); the original outline is kept as `kisumu_boundary_with_lake.geojson`. Kisumu needs three Landsat path/rows (169/060, 169/061, 170/060), so a scene is usable if it covers at least 30 % of the study area and at least 80 % of the covered part is clear (for Nairobi this rule gives the same result as before, because every scene covers the whole county). WorldCover is now mosaicked from all tiles that intersect the study area.
- **D29 (2026-11-10, v2). Wards now come from the IEBC-based HDX layer.** "Administrative Wards in Kenya 1450" (American Red Cross, HDX, CC BY) has exactly 85 Nairobi wards in 17 sub-counties whose names match the IEBC constituencies; every ward name matches the IEBC 2013 election layer (Maron) except Ngei, which that layer lacks. The Maron layer itself sits about 1 km south-west of the population and buildings, so it was used for names only. The ward union covers 695.1 of 705.3 km² (symmetric difference 4.6 % of the county). **The Woodley problem remains:** under these lines Woodley/Kenyatta Golf Course still holds 133,937 WorldPop people (68 % of Kibra sub-county), while the layer's own 2009 census count is 35,355. Building footprints show that part of Kibera's dense fabric does fall inside Woodley under these lines (4,310 buildings under 60 m², more than any other Kibra ward), so the lines are not obviously wrong; most of the gap looks like WorldPop's allocation. Woodley stays in the coolest fifth of wards (F8). Listed in QUESTIONS.md.
- **D30 (2026-11-10, v2). Comparison areas replaced (F3).** City centre = the Nairobi Central ward polygon (3.15 km²; Uhuru Park, Central Park and City Park all lie outside it). Industrial Area = 10 touching OSM `landuse=industrial` polygons merged (4.3 km², ids in `analysis/tables/industrial_area_osm_ids.csv`). Karura and the Arboretum unchanged. **F3 old → new:** city centre 38.8 → 39.1 °C; Industrial Area 41.3 → 41.5 °C; both together 40.5 → 40.7 °C; Karura and Arboretum 27.6 °C (unchanged); gap 12.8 → 13.1 °C. The v1 circles are in `data/processed/_old/comparison_areas_v1.geojson`.
- **D31 (2026-11-10, v2). Roof check result: caveat applies (F16).** The Landsat emissivity used in the five dense, almost treeless wards (median 0.966) is the same as the county median (0.965) and higher than Nairobi Central (0.960). The product therefore does not account for metal roofs there, and those wards may be hotter than shown. Each 0.01 of emissivity overestimated lowers the reading by about 0.7 °C. The web ward layer carries `roof_caveat = true` for wards with at least 75 % built-up and under 5 % tree cover (32 wards), and the ward cards and explorer show the caveat for them.
- **D32 (2026-11-10, v2). Basemaps.** Tested `heat-diff` over Mapbox Light, Satellite Streets and Dark at zooms 10, 12 and 14 (`outputs/screenshots/v2/basemap_tests/`). Light keeps the diverging colours truest and the labels clearest; Satellite at 0.75 adds street texture but darkens the ramp; Dark adds nothing, because the heat layer covers it at 0.9. Choice: **Light** (`light-v11`) for county-wide data chapters at 0.9 opacity, **Satellite Streets** (`satellite-streets-v12`) for imagery and zoomed chapters with data at 0.75; no dark variant. Classic styles were chosen over Mapbox Standard because they expose their symbol layers, so data can go beneath the first label layer. Every basemap label is restyled to dark text with a 1.5 to 1.8 px white halo; POI, transit, airport and natural-point labels are hidden.
- **D33 (2026-11-10, v2). Mapbox satellite tiles refused (HTTP 429), Sentinel-2 fallback.** From the first test, every Mapbox satellite raster tile request returned HTTP 429 "Too Many Requests", even a single request minutes later; vector styles load normally. This looks like an account-level limit on the raster tiles API. I did not switch provider. Instead, the "Satellite" basemap is Mapbox Light with our own Sentinel-2 true-colour composite (10 m, January to March 2026) beneath its roads and labels. This is set by `VITE_SATELLITE` in `site/.env.local`: `sentinel2` (the default), `mapbox` (Satellite Streets, once the account serves imagery) or `auto` (probe one tile per session). I made `sentinel2` the default instead of `auto`, because the probe's refused request shows as a console error on every page load. QUESTIONS 9.
- **D34 (2026-11-10, v2). Tiles.** WebP XYZ tiles made in Python (`src/web/tiles.py`): values are resampled into each Web Mercator tile (area average when zoomed out, bilinear when zoomed in) and then classed, so class edges are smooth curves instead of 30 m or 100 m squares; land cover uses mode and nearest neighbour. County layers z9 to 15, GHSL z9 to 14, Sentinel-2 z9 to 16; the map overzooms with linear resampling. Cells with no clear reading in any scene stay transparent (0.4 % of the county); they are not filled. Absolute surface temperature uses breaks at the county's 10th, 20th, 40th, 60th, 80th and 90th percentiles (seven classes need six breaks). Population uses quintile breaks of populated 100 m cells, with under 100 people per km² left transparent. Growth counts a cell as built-up from 25 % built surface. Vector tiles: tippecanoe directories, uncompressed; tippecanoe skips empty tiles, so empty positions inside the county bounds get zero-byte `.pbf` files (a valid empty tile) to avoid 404s. Vite runs in `appType: 'mpa'` so a missing file returns 404 instead of `index.html`.
- **D35 (2026-11-10, v2). Diverging ramp check.** Checked with `outputs/qa/check_diverging_ramp.py` (CIELAB, and colour-blindness simulation after Machado et al. 2009 at full severity). Lightness L* rises from each end to the neutral midpoint (31, 50, 73, 87, **95**, 85, 74, 53, 35), so the middle class reads as "about the same". Smallest colour difference between neighbouring classes: ΔE 14.3 (normal vision), 12.3 (protanopia), 14.7 (deuteranopia), 14.9 (tritanopia). The −4 to −6 class and the +4 to +6 class stay far apart: ΔE 96.9 normal, 66.9 protanopia, 84.5 deuteranopia. All checks pass, so no steps changed. Class shares over the county: 45.0 % blue, 10.8 % neutral, 44.2 % orange or red, which passes legibility rule 2.
- **D36 (2026-11-10, v2). One shared story map.** Story blocks A and B use one Mapbox map that moves between the two sticky frames, so the page creates at most four maps (story, two for the season swipe, explorer), plus one more only when the explorer's Compare mode is opened.
- **D37 (2026-11-10, v2). Animations play by themselves (exception to "no autoplay").** Stephane asked for moving maps like NASA's wind animation (design brief section 5). Animated maps are muted and looping, play only while at least half on screen, pause when scrolled away, and have a visible Pause/Play button. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` they show the poster and wait for Play. Without JavaScript the browser's own controls show and nothing plays. Hand-coding test: a moving sequence of real satellite mornings shows "the same places, morning after morning" faster than any static figure, so it earns its place.
- **D38 (2026-11-10, v2). "A day of heat" uses CLMS hourly LST, sharpened with Landsat.** `CDSE_CLIENT_ID` and `CDSE_CLIENT_SECRET` are set in `.env` (checked by a script printing only "set" or "missing"), so I took the CLMS path: Global Land Surface Temperature hourly v3 (~3 km), Sentinel Hub collection `byoc-12225aec-26dd-4e2c-bbfd-994c253c1ba8`, for 27 January 2026, the hottest clear Landsat morning of the 2026 hot season. Sharpening is my own implementation: hourly CLMS interpolated to 30 m, plus the same morning's Landsat residual (Landsat minus its average over each CLMS cell) scaled by the day's heating (0 at dawn, 1 at the overpass hour). The Zenodo record for Zlinszky (2026) could not be retrieved through search, so I followed only the general idea named in the prompt and cite it as given. Check at the overpass hour: CLMS minus Landsat averaged to the CLMS cells, bias −1.75 °C, RMSE 2.69 °C, r 0.88 (190 cells). One hour (15:00 Nairobi time) is mostly cloud. Where that morning's Landsat scene was cloudy, no fine detail is added; where CLMS is cloudy, the map shows no colour. ERA5-Land was not needed.
- **D39 (2026-11-10, v2). Calendar heatmap without numbers in its cells.** The brief asks for numbers printed in each heatmap cell. That works for the two tables (17 × 6 and 20 × 5 cells), but the calendar has about 1,400 days in cells of 8 to 20 px, where numbers cannot be read. Its values are given by tap, click or the arrow keys in a line under the calendar, and the data is downloadable as CSV. The calendar's diverging steps are narrower (±0.25, 1, 2, 3 °C) than the map's, because daily air temperature varies less than surface temperature.
- **D40 (2026-11-10, v2). Sentinel-2 composite.** Sentinel-2 L2A from Planetary Computer, January to March 2026, 11 days with under 40 % scene cloud (23 tiles), median of pixels the scene classification marks as vegetation, bare soil or water, offset of 1,000 removed (processing baseline 04.00 and later). Stretch: 1st to 99.5th percentile per band, gamma 1.45 (true colour) and 1.1 (false colour, bands 12, 8A, 4). The first, dask-based version stalled on network reads; the second reads one day at a time. The 10 m GeoTIFFs (45 to 141 MB) are git-ignored and rebuilt by `src/pipeline/s8_sentinel2.py`; the web tiles are committed.
- **D41 (2026-11-10, v2). What changed against v1's look, after comparing with `nasa/08` and `ours_v1/02`.** (1) Heat shown as a difference from the city median on the diverging ramp (D35), so about 45 % of the county is blue and 44 % orange or red, instead of one red ramp. (2) Values are smoothed between cells (D34), so no square pixels at zoom 13. (3) Data goes beneath the first basemap label layer, and every label has dark text with a white halo; ward names are our own bold, upper-case layer from zoom 11 (zoom 10 in the ward chapter), sorted so larger wards win label collisions. (4) Opacity 0.9 over Light and 0.75 over imagery: at 0.75 the street pattern shows through without washing out the ramp. (5) Population moved from grey blocks to a smooth purple quintile ramp; growth shows only new built-up land in red over the 1990 city in grey. (6) Legends are vertical lists or a diverging strip with five ticks, so labels cannot collide. The `nasa/08` look (crisp categorical colours, readable place names over data) is matched on land cover and heat. One thing still falls short: Mapbox's own satellite imagery, which is sharper than our 10 m Sentinel-2 above zoom 15 (D33).

## D42 to D47: fixes after Stephane's review of v2 (10 November 2026, made by Claude directly)

- **D42 Imagery.** Mapbox satellite tiles return HTTP 429 for this account on every request (styles and street tiles work), so it is an account restriction, not traffic. Imagery now comes from Sentinel-2 cloudless 2023 by EOX (10 m, cloud-free, covers the whole region; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, attribution on the map). The site probes one Mapbox satellite tile per session and switches to Mapbox imagery automatically once the account is fixed (`VITE_SATELLITE=auto`, the new default).
- **D43 One style, no reloads.** Every map uses Mapbox Light with an imagery layer shown or hidden. Switching basemap no longer calls `setStyle`, which is what made the story map blank out and redraw between chapters. Satellite is now the default basemap for the story, the swipe map and the explorer.
- **D44 Nairobi stands out.** Everything outside the county is dimmed; the county outline is thicker; maps cannot pan beyond the Nairobi region (max bounds) or zoom out past 9. Every map has a scale bar and a coordinate readout.
- **D45 Explorer.** The mouse wheel scrolls the page (Ctrl or Cmd plus wheel zooms the map; two fingers on phones). One data layer at a time, with buildings allowed on top, so the chosen layer (for example Sentinel-2) is never hidden under the heat layer. The address bar only changes when the reader presses Copy link; a saved view outside Nairobi is ignored.
- **D46 Look.** White page; brand blue rgb(63,121,173) for the header bar, card rules, legend card top border and selected buttons; NASA-style legend card with a collapse button. Line chart rgb(62,7,81) with the El Niño period in rgb(121,193,233). The swipe map now compares the satellite image with the heat layer ("What lies under the heat"), because the season pair already sits above it with numbers.
- **D47 Animations.** Re-rendered with `src/animations/render_nasa.py` as plain data plots in the style of NASA's HRRR and TEMPO animations: title, "Valid:" time stamp, latitude and longitude axes, county and ward outlines, colour bar with units, credit; blue-to-red ramp with cool end rgb(62,77,183). The day of heat now shows the raw CLMS hourly values (about 3 km) over the Nairobi region, without Landsat sharpening. The day-by-day calendar was replaced with a month-by-year table (47 cells, value printed in each) that keeps the El Niño bracket. Heatmap tables no longer stretch to the page width. The autoplay rule in DESIGN_RULES.md now allows looping data animations (Stephane's change).
- Old source files and media are in `_trash/v2_before_claude_fixes/` and `_trash/media_v2/`.
